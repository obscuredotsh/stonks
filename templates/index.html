About this strategy — how entry, stop, and target are calculated

This is NOT a chart-pattern detector. It does not look for cup-and-handle, reverse head-and-shoulders, double bottoms, flags, wedges, or Knoxville Divergence. Nothing in the code recognises a shape on the price plot.

What it actually does: it's a long-biased, multi-indicator consensus trend-following system with a volatility-adaptive stop and a fixed 1:3 risk-reward target. Closest in spirit to:

  • Stan Weinstein — Stage Analysis (only buy stocks in Stage 2 uptrends, above the 200-day moving average) → the EMA200 voter
  • William O'Neil — CAN SLIM (breakouts on heavy volume from a base) → the Vol+Break voter
  • J. Welles Wilder (1978) — RSI, ATR, ADX (momentum, volatility, trend-strength) → RSI(14) voter, 2×ATR stop, ADX(14) gate
  • Olivier Seban / Indian retail — Supertrend (10, 3.0) ATR-based dynamic trend filter → SuperTrend voter
  • Donchian / Turtle Traders — 20-day breakout → the Vol+Break voter (combined with volume)

A trade is fired only when (a) at least 2 of the 7 voters' net opinion is positive (or negative), AND (b) ADX(14) ≥ 20 — meaning the market is actually trending, not chopping sideways.

How each price in the trade plan is calculated

LevelFormulaWhy
Entry today's daily close The candle whose close fired the signal. In live trading you'd place the order at tomorrow's open and accept some slippage.
Stop entry − 2 × ATR(14) Volatility-adaptive — wider for choppy stocks, tighter for steady ones. ATR is the average daily true range over 14 days. Not a support level or chart-pattern stop.
Target entry + 3 × (entry − stop) Fixed 1:3 risk-reward. Exactly 3× the rupee distance from entry to stop. Not a measured-move pattern projection or Fibonacci extension.

Tip for better results: after entry, replace the fixed target with a Chandelier trailing stop — highest high since entry − 3×ATR(14) — and ratchet it up daily. Backtests in documentation.md §10 show that flips the strategy from break-even to PF ~30 (PF ~4 after stress-testing 5× costs). The UI doesn't auto-trail; you'd manage it yourself in the broker.

How are Open trades and Summary different?

🎯 Open trades (above)

A memory list of every BUY/SELL signal the scanner has fired since you opened the page (or before — stored in your browser's localStorage). Each row sticks around until price hits its stop or target — at which point you get a macOS alert. Paper trades only — not in your Groww account.

📊 Summary (below)

The raw output of the most recent scan. One row per symbol you scanned — including WAIT and CHOPPY verdicts that never get tracked. Re-rendered every scan; previous contents are thrown away.
Example: Summary scans 26 symbols → shows 26 rows (BUY/SELL/WAIT/CHOPPY). Of those, only the BUY/SELL signals get added to Open trades (e.g. 20 rows). Open trades also keeps yesterday's signals that aren't in today's scan.
